Shopper Roles — Who Buys

Not who uses the diaper — who decides and buys it. This section draws the purchase as a three-stage chain (decide → buy → execute) and shows the category's defining fact: for most households, all three collapse onto one person — the mother.

Mothers decide; fathers & family often execute the purchase.

The category runs on a single-decider model: the mother researches, selects, and — in most households — also buys. Father participation is real but transactional — placing the order on the brand and pack the mother already fixed. This tightens in nuclear, online-buying Metro / Tier-1 homes, where no elder moderates the choice.

▼ Memory-led repeat, betrayed by consistency
"This is my second time purchasing Bumtum. When I bought it earlier the quality was good — but this time the quality is very poor." Amazon · 2nd-time mom — the risk in a memory-led repeat: batch inconsistency breaks it
Brand implication → There is effectively one target for the purchase — the mother. Win her and the buy follows; the father just executes her choice, so male-targeted "who buys" messaging is largely wasted spend. The cleanest audience is the nuclear, online-buying Metro / Tier-1 mother — no elder to override her. And because repeat is memory-led, batch-to-batch consistency is a purchase-protection issue, not just QC: the Bumtum "this time poor" moment is exactly how a won buyer is lost.
Coverage 78%. Basis: consumer voices with an identifiable caregiver-role cue. Role split is robust for mothers; father/other buyer roles are directional.
