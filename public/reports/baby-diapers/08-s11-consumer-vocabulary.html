Consumer Vocabulary

The words parents actually use, ranked by share of vocabulary mentions and split into what each word literally means vs the emotional charge it carries — because that emotional column is the brief for every pack claim and ad line.

Parents speak performance, not jargon — skin-safety words dominate.

The lexicon is functional and outcome-driven, not aspirational: skin-safety words (rash-free, soft) lead, containment (no-leak, absorption) follows, sleep-value (overnight) close behind. "Cottony" and "langot" form a cultural bridge from cloth heritage to disposable acceptance. Every emotional charge below is grounded in an observable baby state, not an abstract quality claim.

The same words — in praise vs in complaint (real corpus)
▲ The lexicon, stacked in praise
"Too good diapers in every terms. Leak proof. Extra soft. Anti rash. Extra absorb. Size is perfect. Much better than pampers." Flipkart · MamyPoko user · mid-premium — every top term in one breath
▼ The same words, as failure
"Every diaper leaked… The absorbency is very poor, holding capacity is disappointing… Although the material feels soft, the overall performance is below standard." Amazon · Himalaya user · mass — absorption failure is the dominant 1-star driver
Brand implication → Communicate in the dominant register: rash-free + soft + overnight, grounded in observable outcomes (never abstract "quality"). Lovingle already owns two high-value cues in its own reviews — the colour-change indicator (4.1%) and "premium feel at affordable price" value framing — so lead the pack front with rash-free/soft, badge the change-indicator as the premium-over-generic reason, and let "cottony" bridge the cloth-heritage trust.
Coverage 78%. Basis: share of all identified vocabulary mentions across 13 platforms, 2023–2026.
